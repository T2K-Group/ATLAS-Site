Digital Chain of Custody

What Is a Digital Chain of Custody for Physical Assets?

A practical guide to turning location, movement and operational events into a useful record of a physical asset’s journey—before, during and after an important operation.

In ATLAS, digital chain of custody means creating a retained electronic record of the movement, status and operational context of a physical asset. It is different from a simple live GPS location, a traditional paper log and the use of “chain of custody” for digital-forensics evidence.

Knowing where a critical asset is right now can answer an urgent question. Knowing where it has been, what happened around it and how the record was handled can answer the questions that follow.

Beyond a dot on a map

Basic asset tracking is usually centred on the latest position. That position can reduce uncertainty, but it says little about the journey that led there. If an asset arrived late, moved away from an expected location or was involved in an incident, a current location alone cannot explain the sequence.

A digital chain of custody adds continuity and context. It brings together an identified asset, its recorded movements, relevant device or site events, and accountable user actions. The result is an operational history that can be searched and reviewed rather than reconstructed from memory, spreadsheets and phone calls.

ATLAS is not simply a GPS tracker. It is a digital chain-of-custody platform built around the complete asset journey.

A practical definition

A digital chain of custody is a chronological record that helps an authorised team understand the location, movement and operational context of a defined asset over time. It complements the organisation’s physical custody processes; it does not replace staff responsibilities, documented handovers or the controls required for a particular operation.

The word digital matters because relevant events can be captured consistently and made available to authorised users in a shared platform. The phrase chain of custody matters because the history is connected to a known asset and can support accountability across its journey.

Important distinction: an ATLAS record can support operational review, reporting and investigation. Whether any record is admissible or sufficient for a particular legal, regulatory or evidential purpose depends on the circumstances and should be assessed independently.

How the record is formed

The tracker, connectivity and platform work as one operational path. A tracker travels with the asset and reports its available location and device information. Connectivity carries that information to ATLAS. The platform organises it into a live view and retains it as history for later review and reporting.

Real operations do not always have uninterrupted mobile coverage. ATLAS uses multi-network connectivity designed to improve resilience. Where connectivity is temporarily unavailable, location information can be retained and uploaded when connectivity returns. The live view may be delayed during that gap, but the retained information can help preserve the recorded journey.

What this changes operationally

A shared picture

Instead of asking multiple people to confirm locations by phone, an authorised operations team can use a common view of current asset positions and status. Staff can spend more time handling exceptions and less time compiling routine updates.

Earlier awareness

Sites and geofences add meaning to location. Teams can see whether an asset is at an expected operational location, in transit or outside a defined site, then decide what follow-up is appropriate.

Faster investigation

When something unexpected happens, historical movement and event information provides a starting point. A reviewer can select the asset, inspect the sequence and use reporting tools to assemble relevant information.

More consistent accountability

Audit activity can capture user and system actions alongside the asset record. This gives administrators a clearer view of what changed, when it changed and which account initiated an action.

Why elections are a strong example

Election operations involve critical assets moving between multiple locations within a defined timeframe. Existing processes, trained staff and physical controls remain central. Digital chain of custody can add operational visibility without displacing those arrangements.

An operations team can monitor election assets in transit, confirm that assets have reached expected destinations, retain historical movement information and refer back to the timeline if an incident needs investigation. The same principles can support election-asset visibility without replacing established physical custody procedures.

What makes the record useful?

More data is not automatically better. A useful digital chain of custody should be designed around decisions the team may need to make. Before deployment, organisations should define:

  • which assets are important enough to monitor;
  • how each tracker will be associated with an asset;
  • which sites, destinations or journey stages matter;
  • who needs live visibility and who may review historical information;
  • which events require an operational response;
  • how long information should be available, subject to approved policies; and
  • how digital records complement existing physical and procedural controls.

That preparation turns tracking data into an operational tool. The platform then supports a simple progression: see what is happening, understand the history and produce a record when review is needed.

Related terms

Asset chain of custody describes the connected record and controls around a physical asset’s journey. Asset movement history is the chronological location record. An asset tracking audit trail adds relevant user, system and operational events for later review.

Frequently asked questions

Is digital chain of custody the same as GPS tracking?

No. GPS can provide a reported position. A physical-asset chain of custody connects location with asset identity, movement history, operational context and accountable access.

Does a digital record replace physical custody controls?

No. It complements staff responsibilities, handovers, seals, transport procedures and any controls required for the operation.

What happens when connectivity is interrupted?

Live visibility may pause. ATLAS trackers can retain location information during a temporary service gap and upload it when connectivity returns, helping preserve the historical journey.
