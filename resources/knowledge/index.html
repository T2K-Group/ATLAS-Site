What is a digital chain of custody?

A location tells you where an asset is. A digital chain of custody helps you understand where it has been, what happened around it and which people or systems took action.

Our introductory guide explains the difference between basic tracking and an operational record, the signals that make that record useful, and how teams can apply it during routine monitoring, incident review and audit.

  • Location and movement history
  • Sites, geofences and event context
  • User actions and accountable changes
  • Reporting for review and investigation

Guide overview

Four layers make location operationally useful.

Digital chain of custody is not a single sensor reading. It is a structured view of an asset’s journey that retains enough context for day-to-day decisions and later review.

  1. 01
    Identity

    Connect the physical asset to a known tracker and an accountable operational record.

  2. 02
    Movement

    Record current and historical position rather than relying on a one-time location check.

  3. 03
    Context

    Combine sites, geofences, condition readings and device events to explain the journey.

  4. 04
    Accountability

    Retain user and system activity so changes, exports and decisions can be reviewed.
